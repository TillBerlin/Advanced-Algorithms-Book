\chapter{Randomized Algorithms}
\label{ch:randomized}

Randomness buys simplicity and, sometimes, speed. This chapter collects the
probabilistic toolbox, distinguishes the flavours of randomized algorithm, and
then does the two things the course asks of you: solving $2$-list colouring
exactly, and using it inside a randomized $O(1.5^n)$ algorithm for
$3$-colouring.

\begin{goals}
  \goalitem{use Markov's and Chernoff's inequalities and the union bound}
  \goalitem{distinguish Monte Carlo and Las Vegas algorithms and one- from two-sided error}
  \goalitem{explain constant one-sided error and amplify it by repetition}
  \poolgoal{define \lang{2-List Coloring} and show that it can be solved in polynomial time}
  \poolgoal{describe a randomized constant one-sided error algorithm for \lang{3-Coloring} a graph with running time $O(1.5^n)$}
  \poolgoal{define the \lang{Directed $k$-Path} problem and acyclic graphs, and
        show that \lang{Directed $k$-Path} can be solved in polynomial time on
        acyclic graphs}
  \poolgoal{give a randomized constant one-sided error algorithm for
        \lang{Directed $k$-Path} with running time $O(k^k \cdot n^c)$}
\end{goals}
