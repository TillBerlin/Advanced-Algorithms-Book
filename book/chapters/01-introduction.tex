\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

This chapter sets the stage. We fix notation, recall the complexity-theoretic
background the rest of the book assumes, and lay out the four strategies for
dealing with computationally hard problems that structure Parts~III--V. Nothing
here is deep; the point is to agree on a common language.

\section*{Learning goals}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Learning goals}

After this chapter you should be able to:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item state what is assumed as background and what is not;
  \item use $O$, $\Theta$, $\Omega$ and in particular $\Ostar$ notation correctly;
  \item explain informally what a word RAM is and why we usually compute in that model;
  \item recall the definitions of \textbf{P}, \textbf{NP}, NP-hardness and NP-completeness;
  \item name the four standard responses to NP-hardness and say when each is appropriate.
\end{itemize}
