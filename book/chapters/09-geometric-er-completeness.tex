\chapter{\texorpdfstring{Geometric $\ER$-Completeness}{Geometric exists-R-Completeness}}
\label{ch:geometric-er}

With \textsc{ETR-AM} in hand we can prove $\ER$-completeness for problems that
mention no logic at all. The tools are geometric: point--line duality lets us
move between incidence statements about points and about lines, and geometric
intersection graphs let us hide arbitrary algebraic constraints inside an
innocent-looking graph recognition problem.

\begin{goals}
  \poolgoal{11.1}{describe point--line duality}
  \poolgoal{11.2}{show the above--below property of point--line duality}
  \poolgoal{11.3}{define the cyclic order of a point with respect to a point set, and describe what it corresponds to in the dual}
  \poolgoal{11.4}{define geometric intersection graphs, and unit disk graphs in particular}
  \poolgoal{11.5}{show that deciding whether a given graph is a unit disk intersection graph is $\ER$-complete}
  \poolgoal{11.6}{show that \textsc{Optimal Curve Straightening} is $\ER$-complete}
\end{goals}
