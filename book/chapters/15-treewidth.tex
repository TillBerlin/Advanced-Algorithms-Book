\chapter{Treewidth}
\label{ch:treewidth}

Trees are easy. Treewidth measures how far a graph is from being a tree, and
almost every problem that is easy on trees stays easy on graphs of bounded
treewidth. This is the single most useful structural parameter in the book.

\section*{Learning goals}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Learning goals}

After this chapter you should be able to:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item define tree decompositions and treewidth;
  \item determine the treewidth of trees, cycles, series--parallel graphs and grids;
  \item prove that every clique is contained in a bag of every tree decomposition;
  \item construct a tree decomposition of a $p \times q$ grid of width at most $p$;
  \item design dynamic programming algorithms over a nice tree decomposition;
  \item state Courcelle's theorem and explain what it does and does not give you.
\end{itemize}
