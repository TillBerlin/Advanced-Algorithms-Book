\chapter{Planar Graphs}
\label{ch:planar}

Planarity is the prototypical example of structure that an algorithm can
exploit. From Euler's formula we get sparsity, from sparsity colouring
algorithms, and from the separator theorem subexponential algorithms for
problems that are NP-hard in general --- including on planar graphs themselves.

\section*{Learning goals}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Learning goals}

After this chapter you should be able to:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item state and prove Euler's formula;
  \item derive $m \le 3n-6$ and the existence of a vertex of degree at most $5$;
  \item define minors and state Kuratowski's and Wagner's theorems;
  \item prove that planar graphs are $5$-colourable;
  \item give an elementary proof that $K_{3,3}$ is not planar;
  \item state the planar separator theorem and use it to solve $3$-colouring in $2^{O(\sqrt n)}$ time;
  \item prove that $3$-colouring remains NP-hard on planar graphs.
\end{itemize}
